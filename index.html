Good to know before you go

General travel guidance for the Philippines. Always confirm details with official sources.

Most places are easiest from about November to May, when it is drier. Some activities follow their own seasons: surf spots like Siargao, Baler, and Catanduanes have their own swells, whale sharks in Donsol are usually seen from about November to June, and waterfalls look fullest in the wetter months. Each destination page lists its best months.

Typhoon season runs roughly June to November. Boats and flights to islands can be cancelled at short notice, so keep your schedule flexible, buffer a day before flights home, and follow official weather updates from PAGASA.

Cities have ATMs and card payments, but small islands and remote towns often have neither. Withdraw cash before you go and carry small bills for tricycles, entrance fees, and food stalls.

Expect a mix of flights, buses, vans, ferries, jeepneys, tricycles, and habal-habal motorbike taxis. Travel between islands takes longer than the map suggests, so allow extra time and book popular ferries and tours early.

Many places charge entrance, environmental, or guide fees, and some need permits, such as Mt. Pulag, Mt. Apo, and the Puerto Princesa Underground River. Fees and rules change, so check with the local tourism office before you travel.

Don't touch or stand on coral, don't feed wildlife, use reef-safe sunscreen, carry your trash out, and hire local guides and boatmen. Choose operators that follow marine and wildlife guidelines.

Conditions vary, especially in parts of Mindanao and the Sulu archipelago. Check current government travel advisories and ask local operators before booking.
